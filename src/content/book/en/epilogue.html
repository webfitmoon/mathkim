If you have read this book from beginning to end, most of the tools you need are now in your hands: circling conditions, solving by hand, dissecting past papers, connecting concepts, managing time, and spotting the traps in each course. Their names differ, but they all converge on the Five Stages of Decoding.

Remember this. Not one sentence in this book demanded “innate talent.” From beginning to end, it has spoken only of method and the willingness to try again. In a Cambridge lecture room, in an essay-based admissions examination against odds of more than a hundred to one, and at Hyunwoo’s desk after he received Grade 4 and closed his bedroom door, only those two things changed. Talent did not.

Hours of study may not turn straight into marks. But if you can solve a problem today that stopped you yesterday, that progress is yours. Whatever number is on your report card now, it is closer to a starting point than a limit.

Open a problem book tonight and solve the first question all the way through by hand, not by eye. Circle the conditions. Write what is given and what is required in the margin. That one question is your first step toward making the method in this book your own.

— Sungmin Kim
